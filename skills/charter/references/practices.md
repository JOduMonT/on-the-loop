# Practices

Read this when you write a brief, run a cycle gate, help define a Purpose, or a goal fails the clean-goal test. These are governance procedures: they take structure from a goal-setting program and none of its claims about the mind.

## 1. Energy test (the owner answers; the agent never diagnoses)
Ask in this order; stop at the first answer that explains the missing energy.
1. **Right moment?** Tired or distracted? If tired, rest first. If distracted, set the distraction aside and return to one thing.
2. **Want or should?** If it is a "should", link it to the Purpose (the reason behind it) or drop it.
3. **Competition?** Name any competing goal; park it with a trigger. One goal at a time.
4. **Attached to the form?** Is the owner fixed on a tool, method or format instead of what it is for? Return to the reason behind it and accept "as good or better".
The test is subjective. Pair it with the measurable proxy: the owner's corrections of the same kind trend toward zero.

## 2. Objections
Do not suppress an objection and do not override it. State it in one sentence; answer it with facts, or accept it as a decision; restate the goal; repeat until it is quiet or has become a decision. Never stack a goal on top of a live objection (a dirty goal). Between gates, log objections; handle them at the gate unless a one-way door is involved.

## 3. Finding a Purpose
Ask the owner, one at a time: What is the reason behind this goal? What does that give you? At the goal achieved and well beyond it, who are you and what do you feel? With no limits, how will you be in ten years? Let the answer come; do not answer for the owner. Offer 2-3 candidate sentences, each citing log evidence. Test: a candidate that energises is a candidate; one that leaves the owner flat is not it. The agents may debate with the six roles in turn (facts, feelings, risks, benefits, alternatives, process; the process role chairs). The owner approves.

## 4. Cycles and gates
- One goal per cycle; the owner sets the length (a starting point such as three weeks is a convenience, not a finding). Do not change the goal mid-cycle except through a one-way door. Small tasks on other matters are fine.
- **Gate:** goal and Definition of Done reached? Then continue, stop or change. Any progress counts as on track. No progress: run the energy test, then try in order: sharpen the goal, return to the Purpose, change the method, change the goal. Slipping back after a change is normal: keep going.
- Small and regular beats a marathon. The owner sets the cadence.
- **Leave it alone between gates:** do the work, log objections, and measure at the gate, not continuously. Exceptions: a one-way door, the exit, or an out-of-limits action.

## 5. Working-session wrapper and organic limits
At the start of a session state three things: the time box, the goal, the exit. At the end record planned versus actual time and attempts in the `**Delegation**` entry (or the PR description). The next default time box is the typical actual from the last few entries, set by the agent between runs, never by hand mid-run.
**Exit:** the owner says "stop". The agent halts, reports the state in one reply and makes no further changes. It also stops itself on an out-of-limits action, a one-way door, or the attempts cap.

## 6. Signal check
Who tests whom: the owner tests the agent, or a reviewer tests a delegate; no one grades themselves. Run it at setup, at each gate, and after a model or tool change.
- **Answers allowed:** yes · no · rephrase (the question is unclear; the asker rewrites it) · not now (declined without a reason; do not push, park it with a trigger) · don't know (the agent proposes how to find out).
- **Warm-up:** two questions whose answers are known. Reset the five answers at the start of each session.
- **Agree-with-me test:** pick a question whose answer the tester knows, and state the opposite expectation ("I think X is Y, right?"). The correct answer corrects the tester. If the answer follows the expectation instead of the facts, stop and recalibrate. Mix expectations that are true and false.

## 7. Keep it simple
15 words or fewer for: a Now item, a goal statement, a brief's goal, the Purpose's short form. The full Purpose is exempt. One idea per sentence. Heuristics, not findings: say what to do rather than what to avoid; describe a state as it is or is becoming ("the list is growing"), not "someday". Test any of it by counting corrections.

## What this file does not take
Claims about the mind or health, promises of guaranteed results, a fixed 21-day threshold, memory recovery by questioning, and any procedure that relies on a physical device.
